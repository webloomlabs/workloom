# S11 — Support, maintenance, infrastructure, documents, and recurring billing

**Depends on:** S4 (the client view), S7b (invoices), S10 (the worker's sweeps)
**State:** done

**Amends the plan:**

- **The four Phase 2 client sections ship together, with recurring billing.**
  The roadmap lists Documents, Recurring invoices, Support & maintenance, and
  Infrastructure as four independent Phase 2 items. They are one slice here
  because they answer one question — what happens to a client after the project
  ships — and because three of them are inert without the fourth: a maintenance
  plan that cannot bill is a note in a text file, and a support ticket with no
  service level is a to-do.
- **Recurring billing raises drafts, never issued invoices.** The roadmap says
  "schedules, generation, and automated sending". Generation is here; sending is
  not. An invoice is a statement to a client, and the last thing to read one
  before it goes out should be a person. Automated sending remains available to
  build when an agency asks for it, behind a per-schedule switch.
- **Infrastructure stores no credentials.** §12 makes encrypted credential
  storage a hard requirement of the infrastructure module. Doing it properly
  needs envelope encryption, a restricted role, and an audit entry on every
  read; doing it carelessly puts secrets in a database backup in plain text.
  The module ships without them, and the schema says why in the file.
- **Tickets share the document numbering sequence.** `document_sequences` gained
  a `ticket` kind rather than a second gapless per-tenant counter being written.
- **A ticket's SLA targets are stamped once, at creation.** Recomputing them
  from the plan on read would let a renegotiated contract rewrite whether last
  month's tickets were answered on time.

## Scope

In:
- **Support**: tickets with type, priority, status, assignee, an internal/external
  conversation, and two service-level clocks with the state of each.
- **Maintenance**: plans with inclusions, service levels, and an optional link to
  the schedule that bills them; visits recording what was actually done.
- **Infrastructure**: domains, hosting, servers, applications, certificates —
  with renewal dates, renewal costs, and an hourly sweep that announces what is
  approaching and marks what has passed.
- **Documents**: files filed against a client, in object storage, with
  short-lived signed downloads and the client-visible flag.
- **Recurring billing**: schedules that raise a draft invoice per period, priced
  by the same calculator as a hand-written one.
- The four client-record sections, which stop being "planned" and start counting.
- Sidebar sections, list and detail pages, and forms for all of the above.

Out:
- Automated sending of generated invoices (above).
- Credential storage on infrastructure assets (above).
- Provider integrations: Cloudflare, Vercel, uptime monitoring, registrar APIs.
- Inbound email to tickets. The conversation model is ready for it; the mail
  ingestion is its own slice.
- The client portal that reads `client_visible` on documents and tickets.
- Recurring **tasks**, which belong with project templates.

## What this slice found

- **A sweep that throws stops every schedule behind it.** All of one
  organization's due schedules are billed in a single transaction, so an
  archived tax rate on one retainer would have aborted the rest of that hour's
  billing. Schedules are now checked for a *blocker* — no lines, an archived
  client, an archived service or tax rate — before anything is attempted, and a
  blocked schedule is reported on its own page rather than silently skipped.
- **Double-billing had to be impossible, not unlikely.** A retried job or a
  double-clicked button is the ordinary case, not the exotic one. The period is
  recorded in `billing_schedule_invoices` with a unique key on
  `(schedule, period_start)`, so the second attempt fails on a constraint, and
  `next_run_on` advances inside the same transaction as the invoice.
- **Stepping the calendar forward drifts.** Adding a month to a clamped 28
  February leaves every later period on the 28th. Periods are computed from the
  start date by index, which is what keeps "the 31st" meaning the 31st.
- **The first response is a database concern.** Stamping it in the service
  would mean every future path that inserts a message — the API, an inbound
  email worker, an import — has to remember to. A trigger on `ticket_messages`
  sets it once, for the first message that is not internal.
- **`waiting_on_client` does not stop the resolution clock.** From the client's
  side the thing still is not fixed. An agency that wants a stopped clock there
  is promising something different, and that belongs in a plan's written terms
  rather than in a hidden rule.
- **Auto-renewing assets are still announced.** "Auto-renew" is a setting at a
  registrar, not a fact; the card on file expires too.

## Notes for later slices

- **The client portal** has everything it needs: `client_visible` on documents,
  `internal` on ticket messages, and plans and visits that are already written
  for a client to read.
- **Inbound email to tickets** should write a `ticket_messages` row and let the
  trigger stamp the response, rather than reimplementing either.
- **Automated sending** is a per-schedule flag plus `invoice.send` in the sweep.
  The generation path already runs as a job actor with no permissions.
- **Provider integrations** fill `infrastructure_assets` from Cloudflare, Vercel,
  and registrars. The columns they need are there; nothing about them requires
  the schema to change.
- **Recurring tasks** belong with project templates, not here: they produce work,
  not invoices.
